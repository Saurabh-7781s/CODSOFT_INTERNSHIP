1. What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering technique in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick a user into revealing information, opening a malicious attachment, transferring money or visiting a harmful website.

Suspicious email signs

  • Unexpected urgency or threats.
  • Sender address does not match the claimed organization.
  • Unexpected attachments or links.
  • Requests for passwords, OTPs or payment details.
  • Spelling, branding or formatting inconsistencies.

Fake login page signs

  • Check the complete domain name.
  • HTTPS alone does not prove legitimacy.
  • Unexpected login prompts reached through email links.
  • Browser warnings or unusual redirects.
  • Requests for information the service normally does not ask for.

Fraudulent website signs

  • Look-alike domains or unusual spelling.
  • Fake countdowns and excessive urgency.
  • Unusual payment instructions.
  • Missing contact/privacy information.
  • Promises that are too good to be true.

2. Common social-engineering techniques

Pretexting

An attacker invents a believable situation to obtain information.

Impersonation

The attacker pretends to be a colleague, bank, delivery service or administrator.

Urgency

The victim is pressured to act before verifying the request.

Quid pro quo

The attacker offers a benefit or service in exchange for information or an action.

Baiting

A tempting file, offer or device is used to encourage unsafe behavior.

Smishing / Vishing

Phishing delivered through SMS or voice calls.

3. Practical security checklist

  1. Pause when a message creates unusual urgency.
  2. Verify the sender using a trusted channel.
  3. Never share passwords, PINs or OTPs.
  4. Navigate to important services using a known bookmark or manually typed address.
  5. Use multi-factor authentication.
  6. Keep browsers, operating systems and security software updated.
  7. Report suspicious messages to the appropriate IT/security team.

4. Case studies

Case A – Account verification email

You receive an email saying your account will be locked in 30 minutes unless you verify your password through a link.

Correct response: Do not use the email link. Open the official service through a known address/bookmark and check independently.

Case B – Fake delivery message

An SMS claims that a parcel cannot be delivered and asks for a small payment through a link.

Correct response: Verify the shipment through the courier's official app/site rather than the SMS link.
